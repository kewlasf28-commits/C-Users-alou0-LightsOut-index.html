04 / Community accountability

Take complaints seriously. Demand proof.

Reports of harassment, intimidation, property intrusion, or unexplained work deserve careful documentation and fair, independent review.

What residents can document

Dates, times, photographs, videos, witnesses, written notices, work orders, property records, and responses from city offices or utilities. Describe what you directly observed, and keep interpretations separate.

What public agencies should provide

Clear explanations of permitted utility work, transparent complaint procedures, timely responses, records-access pathways, and written findings whenever possible.

Important: The defense-contract records on this page do not establish that any named company, program, or individual organized community harassment. The topics appear together as public-accountability issues, not as a proven causal link.

Public records questions worth asking

Who authorized work at a property? Which permit or utility locate covers it? Which company performed it? Who supervised the work? What complaint number was assigned? What documents support the agency's explanation?
